Mark Watney wasn't trying to solve the impossible problem of surviving Mars.
He was solving smaller problems that could keep him alive one more day.
Stranded alone with thirty days of food and four years until rescue, looking at the whole picture meant giving up.
So Watney broke survival into pieces he could actually measure.
Food became a botany experiment.
Water became a chemical reaction.
Communication became an old probe buried in the sand.
He didn't need to know how he would escape.
He only needed to solve the next calculation, the next test, the next decision.
When his greenhouse blew up, he didn't panic—he recalculated.
That’s how Watney survives.
Not by solving one impossible problem, but by solving enough small problems to stay alive.
Sometimes, the way to overcome the impossible is to stop treating it as one problem.
