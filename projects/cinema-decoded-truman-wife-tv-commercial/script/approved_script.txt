Notice how in The Truman Show, Truman’s wife Meryl keeps acting like she’s in a TV commercial? In the middle of serious conversations, she randomly holds up a kitchen knife or a jar of cocoa, smiles at an invisible camera, and recites marketing slogans like: "Why don't you let me fix you some of this new Mococoa drink?" Truman thinks she’s losing her mind, but there’s a dark reason for this. The Truman Show airs twenty-four hours a day, completely uninterrupted. That means there are zero commercial breaks. So how does a billion-dollar television show make its money? Product placement. Everything inside Truman’s world is for sale in the official show catalog. Meryl isn’t just Truman’s wife—she’s a paid actor working on commission. Every time she holds up a product to the hidden cameras, she’s literally running an ad to pay for the broadcast.
