# Fact-Check & Verification Report: Why Is Godzilla Minus One Called Minus One?

## 1. Statement-by-Statement Verification

| Script Statement | Verification Status | Source & Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **"The story takes place in Japan just after World War II."** | ✅ **CONFIRMED** | Canonical setting is late 1945–1947, directly following the Japanese surrender and Tokyo firebombings. |
| **"The war has already left the country devastated."** | ✅ **CONFIRMED** | Depicted accurately in the film: leveled neighborhoods, civilian malnutrition, makeshift shelters. |
| **"Its cities are destroyed, its economy is broken, and people are struggling to rebuild..."** | ✅ **CONFIRMED** | Historically accurate depiction of postwar Tokyo; millions homeless, hyperinflation, food rationing, black markets. |
| **"In other words, Japan is already at zero."** | ✅ **OFFICIAL METAPHOR** | Official director Takashi Yamazaki explanation and Toho press release: Japan had lost everything and was at "zero." |
| **"Then Godzilla appears. And instead of letting the country recover, he causes even more destruction."** | ✅ **CONFIRMED** | Godzilla attacks Ginza in 1947 just as basic civilian life and commuter trains were being restored. |
| **"Japan goes from zero to minus one. That's where the title comes from."** | ✅ **OFFICIAL ETYMOLOGY** | Verbatim confirmed in Toho's official July 2023 press announcement (*ゴジラ-1.0*). |
| **"Godzilla doesn't just destroy a country. He pushes a country that has already lost everything even further into disaster."** | ✅ **VERIFIED THEME** | Core dramatic thesis articulated by Director Yamazaki: an existential blow to a nation already on its knees. |
